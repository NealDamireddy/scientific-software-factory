# Claim-to-evidence review

## Evidence to gather

The claim, its population and time window, and the underlying observations with identity keys.

## Review procedure

1. Translate the request into a quantity the evidence can answer. Identify the population, unit of observation, unit being compared, time window and intended units. Ask what gets one vote in the calculation. For change over time, determine whether the question concerns the same subjects or the populations observed at each time. State the relevant denominator and exclusions.

2. Trace the claim to its records and independently recompute it. Use identity keys for paired measurements. For grouped records, choose weighting from the research question and documented analysis plan. If two interpretations give materially different answers and the context cannot resolve them, give the conditional results or ask the narrow question that distinguishes them. Keep measured values, derived quantities and assumptions distinguishable.

3. Report what the records support, contradict or leave unresolved. Cite the decisive observations or reproducible selection and the lab context that justifies it. Explain what would change the decision and preserve conclusions unaffected by missing evidence. When a claim relies on certification, read the issuing source’s value table, qualifications and applicable revision. In the wording you would release, identify exactly which material or lot, property, value, units and uncertainty the issuer certifies, with any condition that changes its use. Give each other disputed value the status the source assigns it, such as a reference value, an estimate or a control result. If the supplied record only reports measurements, say what it establishes and what document would be needed to claim certification. A shared material name or an agreeing number does not extend the scope of a certificate.

## Expected result

Agreement, disagreement or a missing-evidence assessment with row references.
