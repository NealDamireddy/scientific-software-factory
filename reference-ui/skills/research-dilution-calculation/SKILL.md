# Dilution calculation

## Evidence to gather

Recorded sample response, applicable calibration, dilution factor and reported concentration.

## Review procedure

1. Trace the reported value back to the diluted sample. Identify the repeat run, standard concentration–response pairs, sample response, reporting units, recorded dilution factor and its convention. Distinguish a result for the measured aliquot from a result for the original sample. Use the recorded preparation factor for calculation; a planned or approved factor is evidence of intent.

2. Check whether the measured response falls within the calibrated region. Use the method’s recorded curve and conversion rules. For the illustrative B12 method, locate adjacent standards that bracket the response on a monotonic curve and interpolate linearly. If the bracket is ambiguous, units conflict, or the method requires model parameters that are absent, stop with the missing input. Outside the usable range, inspect whether concentration was withheld and flag an extrapolated numeric report.

3. Show the audit trail in units. In the supplied B12 example, 0.650 AU interpolates to 7.5 µM in the measured aliquot. A recorded twofold dilution gives 15.0 µM in the original sample. Check the report against its stated target and precision; applying the same factor again would give 30.0 µM. Anchor the discrepancy to the reported value, identify the first unsupported step, and retain the calculation as an advisory review.

## Expected result

A cited comparison or a determination that concentration should be withheld.
