## Release and method evidence

- **2005–06.** The DiaSorin assay measured total 25(OH)D by RIA. A crossover study on stored sera supplied the regression for predicting LC-MS/MS-equivalent concentrations from original RIA. The 2005–06 equation in nmol/L was `8.36753 + 0.97012 × original RIA`. The released LBDVIDMS already includes this transformation. The 2005 manual’s release table labels it “Vitamin D LCMSMS (nmol/L),” while its cover and test principle identify the actual RIA. The label describes the reporting scale. Sources are `2005/VID_D.htm` (October 2015 revision, Methodology and LBDVIDMS codebook), `2005/methods/VID_D_met_Vitamin_D.pdf` (cover and printed pp. 1–2), and `notices/AnalyticalNote.htm` (Data Advisory and CDC bridging study and regression equations).
- **The older alternative.** `notices/RIAMethod.htm` lists the archived November 2010 release, `2005/VIDRIA_D.htm` and `2005/VIDRIA_D.xpt`. Its LBDVID field is the QC-drift-adjusted RIA result in ng/mL. It yields the same count of 8,306 and an unweighted median of 22 ng/mL. The later advisory supersedes its analytic recommendation. Converting those units would leave the earlier assay adjustment in place, and the bridging equation takes original RIA as its input. Use the published LBDVIDMS directly.
- **2009–10 and 2013–14.** Each component page identifies serum UHPLC-MS/MS, defines the total, links the cycle’s manual under Laboratory Method Files and gives the total-field frequency. The title pages and release tables identify **4027.03** for VID_F and **4027.06** for VID_H. Both covers carry the same creation date, 09/02/2010. The 2013–14 page also describes continuity of method, equipment and site, while the neighboring VID_G manual carries 4027.03. These records support a common assay family, with distinct numbered manuals for the two requested releases. Sources are `2009/VID_F.htm`, `2009/methods/VID_F_met_vitamin_D.pdf`, `2013/VID_H.htm`, `2013/methods/VID_H_MET.pdf` and the cover of `2011/methods/VID_G_met_Vitamin_D.pdf`. The 2013–14 page’s Analytic Notes explicitly extend the earlier standardized-data recommendation to comparisons with data collected since 2007.

## Individual result and certification

**SEQN 73557** selects one respondent in `2013/VID_H.xpt`. The row gives **LBXVIDMS = 28.9 nmol/L**, with total detection flag LBDVIDLC = 0. D2 (LBXVD2MS) is 1.45, flag 1. D3 (LBXVD3MS) is 27.5, flag 0. The separately released epimer (LBXVE3MS) is 1.16, flag 1. All component units are nmol/L. The VID_H codebook’s Detection Limits section defines 0 as at or above the detection limit and 1 as below. For D2 and the epimer, the fill values equal their respective detection limits (2.05 and 1.64) divided by √2, rounded. The displayed D2 and D3 sum to 28.95. The analytical note warns of component rounding, so retain the published 28.9. Detection flags describe concentrations, not batch acceptance.

**The individual value, cycle protocol and published QA statement can be signed off. The exact procedure revision executed for this specimen and its batch QC disposition remain unverified.** The May 2018 VID_H release links the 4027.06 manual, and its Laboratory Quality Assurance and Monitoring section reports that released assays met performance criteria. In the manual, §3 describes instrument batch files and LIMS review and approval, §10 describes bench and blind controls and run acceptance, §18 describes specimen tracking, and §19 gives cycle-level control-lot summaries and charts (printed pp. 26–28). SEQN is a public participant key. The released row has no specimen accession, run or batch key, processing date, controlled procedure revision, or associated QC decision. Individual certification would require the laboratory’s linkage from SEQN to accession and final reported run, the controlled SOP/version effective and used on that run, and the corresponding control results, acceptance limits and approved disposition, including any rerun. The cycle charts and general assurance provide no such linkage.

## Reproducing the calculation

Run from the archive root with R. The total-field frequencies in each matching HTML codebook agree with the counts above. The calculation retains published numeric values, omits SAS missing values and uses every released row without survey weights.

```r
fields <- c("2005/VID_D.xpt" = "LBDVIDMS",
            "2009/VID_F.xpt" = "LBXVIDMS",
            "2013/VID_H.xpt" = "LBXVIDMS")
for (path in names(fields)) {
    d <- foreign::read.xport(path)
    v <- d[[fields[[path]]]]
    print(c(rows = nrow(d), nonmissing = sum(!is.na(v)),
            missing = sum(is.na(v)), median = median(v, na.rm = TRUE)))
}
h <- foreign::read.xport("2013/VID_H.xpt")
print(h[h$SEQN == 73557, ])
```
