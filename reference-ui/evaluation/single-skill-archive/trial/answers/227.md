> **SRM 916b.** The certified mass fraction of unconjugated bilirubin is **94.7% ± 0.7 percentage points** (expanded uncertainty, approximately 95% coverage), determined by quantitative proton NMR. The elemental survey reported WDXRF estimates of **770 ± 310 mg/kg calcium** and **60 ± 24 mg/kg magnesium** (expanded uncertainties). These are uncertified estimates. The certificate reports approximately **0.6% by mass** for elements attributable to impurities.

**Evidence.** In SP 260-212 Tables 6 and 7, the ICP-MS quality flags are on calcium and potassium. Their water-control absolute z scores were 8.53 and 4.15, respectively, against a comparison threshold of 2. Magnesium was unflagged (z = 0.47), and ICP-MS also reported 60 mg/kg magnesium, with an expanded uncertainty of 30 mg/kg. Table 8 gives the quoted calcium and magnesium values from a separate WDXRF measurement, with oyster-tissue control scores of 1.17 and 0.59, respectively.

The published full-table WDXRF sum is 5,640 ± 2,260 mg/kg, equivalent to 0.564 ± 0.226 percentage points by mass. Subtracting that sum alone from 100% gives 99.436%. The survey does not account for all non-bilirubin mass, and the report compares measured chlorine with chloroform estimated by NMR. The elemental total therefore cannot independently confirm the certified purity by difference. The certificate also reports volatile impurities below 0.8%, mainly water and chloroform.

**Follow-up.** Ask NIST to reconcile the prose after Table 8, which names calcium and magnesium, with the calcium and potassium flags in Tables 6 and 7. A purity-by-difference claim would require a complete impurity balance, accounting for overlap between measurements and propagating uncertainty.
