Hold the proposed entry. Publish this wording instead:

> SRM 916b has a certified unconjugated bilirubin mass fraction of 94.7%, with an expanded uncertainty of 0.7 percentage points at approximately 95% coverage, determined by quantitative proton NMR. The WDXRF impurity survey reported calcium at 770 ± 310 mg/kg and magnesium at 60 ± 24 mg/kg (expanded uncertainties). These are survey estimates. [Certificate, pp. 1–2; SP 260-212, Table 8]

ICP-MS flagged calcium and potassium in both the 916b and water-control tables. Their control |z| scores were 8.53 and 4.15, respectively, above the report’s comparison value of 2. Magnesium was unflagged and had a control |z| of 0.47. The WDXRF oyster-tissue control scores for calcium and magnesium were 1.17 and 0.59. The quoted elemental values come from WDXRF, a separate measurement method. Both methods reported 60 mg/kg for magnesium. [SP 260-212, Tables 6–8 and §§3.2–3.3]

The full WDXRF sum of 5,640 mg/kg converts to 0.564% by mass, consistent with the certificate’s approximate 0.6% elemental survey figure. The certified result leaves 5.3 percentage points by subtraction. The elemental survey and the reported volatile fraction below 0.8% do not establish a complete impurity balance, and the report compares measured chlorine with chloroform estimated by NMR. The survey therefore does not independently confirm purity by difference. [Certificate, pp. 1–2; SP 260-212, §3.3 and Table 8]

Ask NIST to reconcile the post–Table 8 reference to calcium and magnesium with the calcium and potassium flags in Tables 6 and 7, and to clarify which WDXRF results the authors intended to prefer. The April 2026 certificate revision records validity and editorial changes and does not resolve that discrepancy. [Certificate, p. 3; SP 260-212, §§3.2–3.3]
