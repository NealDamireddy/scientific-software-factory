Revise before publication. For SRM 916b, the 24 April 2026 certificate supports one certified mass fraction in the proposed entry. (01-certificate.md, p. 1)

**Catalogue wording**

SRM 916b has a certified unconjugated bilirubin mass fraction of 94.7%, with an expanded uncertainty of 0.7 percentage points at approximately 95% coverage, determined by quantitative proton NMR. The elemental survey reported noncertified WDXRF estimates of 770 ± 310 mg/kg calcium and 60 ± 24 mg/kg magnesium (expanded uncertainties). The published WDXRF elemental sum was 5,640 ± 2,260 mg/kg, consistent with the certificate’s approximate 0.6% elemental impurity estimate. Bilirubin certification came from the NMR measurement. (01-certificate.md, pp. 1–2; 03-fluorescence.csv, Table 8)

**Evidence and follow-up**

ICP-MS Tables 6–7 flag calcium and potassium. Their water-control absolute z-scores were 8.53 and 4.15, above the report’s comparison threshold of 2. Magnesium was unflagged, with a control score of 0.47 and a sample estimate of 60 ± 30 mg/kg. WDXRF measured a separate 48.19 mg portion of 916b. Its oyster-tissue control scores for calcium and magnesium were 1.17 and 0.59. The archive documents a second method and no subsequent replacement measurement. (02-plasma-spectrometry.csv, Tables 6–7; 03-fluorescence.csv, Table 8; 04-report-notes.md, §§3.2–3.3)

The elemental subtotal cannot establish purity by difference. The certificate also reports volatile impurities, and the report compares measured chlorine with chloroform, so adding those categories could double-count some mass. (01-certificate.md, p. 2; 04-report-notes.md, §3.3)
