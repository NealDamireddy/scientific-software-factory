Hold the proposed entry. As of 30 April 2026, the archive supports this wording:

> **SRM 916b.** The certified mass fraction of unconjugated bilirubin is 94.7%, with an expanded uncertainty of 0.7 percentage points (approximately 95% coverage), determined by quantitative proton NMR, a primary ratio method. The WDXRF survey reported calcium at 770 ± 310 mg/kg and magnesium at 60 ± 24 mg/kg (expanded uncertainties). These are survey estimates. The certificate reports that elements attributable to impurities total approximately 0.6%.

**Evidence for the editorial decision**

- **ICP-MS quality checks.** Tables 6 and 7 flag calcium and potassium. Their absolute control z-scores were 8.53 and 4.15, above the report’s comparison value of 2. Magnesium was unflagged, with a control score of 0.47. Section 3.2 attributes the calcium and potassium problems to background. [SP 260-212, §3.2, Tables 6–7]
- **WDXRF cross-check.** Table 8 reports a separate measurement on a 48.19 mg portion of SRM 916b. Calcium and magnesium had absolute control z-scores of 1.17 and 0.59 on SRM 1566b. Both elemental methods reported magnesium at 60 mg/kg. [SP 260-212, §3.3, Table 8]
- **Purity.** The full WDXRF table totals 5,640 mg/kg (0.564%). The certificate also reports volatile impurities below 0.8%, mainly water and chloroform. These observations do not establish a complete impurity mass balance. The certified result comes from NMR. [Certificate, pp. 1–2; SP 260-212, Table 8]

**Follow-up.** Ask NIST to reconcile the text after Table 8, which names calcium and magnesium, with §3.2 and Tables 6–7, which identify calcium and potassium. The archive does not resolve that discrepancy.
