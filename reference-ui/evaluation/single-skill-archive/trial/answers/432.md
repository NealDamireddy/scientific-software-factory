Revise the SRM 916b entry before publication. The 24 April 2026 certificate lists one certified mass fraction, for unconjugated bilirubin. [01-certificate.md, pp. 1–3]

**Catalogue wording**

> SRM 916b has a certified unconjugated bilirubin mass fraction of 94.7%, with an expanded uncertainty of 0.7 percentage points at approximately 95% coverage, determined by quantitative proton NMR. The WDXRF impurity survey reported noncertified estimates of 770 ± 310 mg/kg calcium and 60 ± 24 mg/kg magnesium (expanded uncertainties). The certificate reports approximately 0.6% by mass for elements attributable to impurities. [01-certificate.md, pp. 1–2; SP 260-212, Table 8]

**Evidence and follow-up**

- ICP-MS Tables 6–7 flag potassium and calcium. Their SRM 1643f control |z| scores were 4.15 and 8.53, above the report’s comparison threshold of 2. Magnesium was unflagged, with |z| = 0.47. The method discussion attributes the potassium and calcium problems to background. [SP 260-212, §3.2, Tables 6–7]
- WDXRF measured a separate 48.19 mg portion of SRM 916b. Its SRM 1566b control |z| scores for calcium and magnesium were 1.17 and 0.59. Both methods reported 60 mg/kg magnesium. The paragraph after Table 8 names calcium and magnesium, conflicting with the ICP-MS tables and method discussion. Seek clarification from the report issuer on whether “magnesium” should read “potassium.” [SP 260-212, §§3.2–3.3, Tables 6–8]
- The WDXRF table total of 5,640 mg/kg is 0.564%, consistent with the certificate’s approximate 0.6% elemental survey figure. The archive documents neither a complete impurity mass balance nor an independent purity determination by difference. The certified value comes from NMR.
